Leaders Light the Sector

How Fortinet and CrowdStrike lit up a weak Security Software group from the bottom of their bases — the case that individual stock momentum leads sector momentum, not the other way around.

The thesis

Individual stock momentum is primary. Group momentum is secondary.

During a correction, the stocks that resist the downside best are quietly telling you who the next leaders are. When the follow-through day arrives, buying the strongest names from the strongest groups is a high-probability play — that part of the CANSLIM® playbook is not in dispute. But there is a second, subtler truth that this case study exists to prove: as a market transitions into a Power Trend, new leaders emerge from groups that still look weak. If you wait for the industry group to strengthen before you act, you routinely miss the first — and largest — leg of the move.

Fortinet and CrowdStrike are the cleanest example I have on file. Their group, Security Software, was ranked 30th of 200 when they broke out. Their own momentum dragged the group up to 9th. The leaders lit the sector, not the reverse.

Where it starts

The classic rule: strongest stocks, strongest groups, on the FTD

On the 04/08/2026 follow-through day, the top-ranked industry groups were a tight cluster around one theme — the AI hardware build-out. Buying leaders out of that cluster was the high-probability call, and it worked.

So where is the problem? Nowhere — yet. The classic rule is correct on the FTD. The problem is what happens next. A Power Trend is not static: leadership broadens and rotates. New names begin to move weeks before their group ranking reflects it, because a group rank is an average and an average always lags its strongest member. If your only filter is "buy from a top-ranked group," you are structurally late to every leader whose group has not caught up yet.

The case study

FTNT & CRWD — when the stock moves first

On 05/07/2026, Fortinet broke out from the bottom of its base on an enormous institutional volume footprint. Its move pulled CrowdStrike with it the same day. At that moment, their group was ranked 30th of 200.

The proof

The group ranking followed the stocks — 30 → 9

Group strength is the effect here, not the cause. Watch the Security Software ranking climb after the leaders had already moved.

The sequence is the whole point. Stock catalyst → institutional volume footprint → leaders run → group average rises → group rank enters the top 10. Individual momentum ignites first; sector momentum is the smoke, not the spark.

Why it works this way

Five reasons group momentum lags the leader

This is not an anomaly. It is structural — and it repeats.

Group momentum is a lagging indicator

An industry-group rank is an average of every stock in the group. By construction, an average trails its strongest members — the leader has to pull the mean up before the rank moves.

Stocks move on their own catalysts

A powerful story or earnings acceleration — FTNT's AI-cybersecurity demand — can drive a single stock regardless of where its group ranks that week.

Many big winners begin while their group is still weak

FTNT and CRWD broke out from a group ranked 30th. Waiting for the group to look strong would have meant entering after the first, largest leg was over.

Leaders pull their sector up

Sector strength usually follows the leaders. The group rank is downstream of the price action of its best names, not upstream of it.

Relying only on strong groups makes you buy laggards

A group can look strong because of one or two heavy-hitters while the rest are weak. Screen the group first and you risk buying the tired names inside a "strong" group instead of the actual leader.

How to apply it

Two kinds of leadership — and a growth strategy needs both

Recognizing only one of these patterns leaves money on the table. The complete approach watches for both.

Pattern A · Classic

Strong group + strong stock

The textbook CANSLIM® setup: a leading stock inside a leading group, forming bases and pulling back constructively while sector momentum runs for months. Highest-conviction, most durable trades.

Example: SNDK. A data-storage leader riding months of sector momentum — up ∼511% from its pivot in 35 weeks, EPS exploding, group RS at 97.

Pattern B · Emerging

Strong stock in a weak group

The stock catalyst arrives before the group does. Group ranked 30th, but a real story and a real volume footprint trigger the move — and that momentum creates the group's later strength.

Example: FTNT & CRWD. Early entries on the stock signal captured the whole move before the group ranking improved from 30 to 9.

The conclusion

Prioritize the stock's relative strength first, the group's second

The most effective growth strategy leads with individual stock relative strength and uses industry-group strength as confirmation, not as the first gate. A rising leader in a lagging group is not a contradiction to resolve — it is often the highest-reward entry on the board. This is the principle that governs how leadership is monitored and tracked at Trade2Swing.
